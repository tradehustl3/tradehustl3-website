Every skill starts somewhere.

You don't need to have it all figured out. Discover the trades, understand the work, and find a path that fits you.

  • HVAC & Refrigeration

    Heating, cooling & refrigeration

    The work
    Install, maintain, and repair the heating, cooling, ventilation, and refrigeration systems that control temperature and air quality in buildings.
    How to start
    Many start with a 6-month to 2-year program at a technical school or community college. Others train through an apprenticeship that pairs paid on-the-job training with classroom instruction.
    Credentials to know
    • EPA 608Federal rule
    • NATEEmployer-preferred
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred

    Source: BLS — HVAC and refrigeration mechanics and installers

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Electrical

    Power, wiring & controls

    The work
    Install, maintain, and repair power, lighting, communications, and control systems in homes, businesses, and factories.
    How to start
    A high school diploma or equivalent, then most train through a 4- or 5-year paid apprenticeship. A technical-school program can earn credit toward one.
    Credentials to know
    • State electrical licenseState / local
    • NCCEREmployer-preferred
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred

    Source: BLS — Electricians

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Plumbing

    Water, piping & systems

    The work
    Install and repair the pipes, fixtures, and piping systems that carry water and waste through buildings.
    How to start
    A high school diploma or equivalent, then most learn through a 4- or 5-year paid apprenticeship. Some start as helpers or finish a pre-apprenticeship first.
    Credentials to know
    • State plumbing licenseState / local
    • NCCEREmployer-preferred
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred

    Source: BLS — Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Welding & Fabrication

    Metalwork & fabrication

    The work
    Use hand-held or remotely controlled equipment to join, cut, and repair metal parts and products.
    How to start
    Employers usually want a high school diploma plus technical training from a high school program, community college, or welding school, followed by months of on-the-job training. Some employer apprenticeships take beginners.
    Credentials to know
    • AWS Certified WelderEmployer-preferred
    • AWS CWIEmployer-preferred
    • NCCEREmployer-preferred

    Source: BLS — Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Construction

    Building & carpentry

    The work
    Build, repair, and install frameworks and structures made of wood and other materials.
    How to start
    A high school diploma or equivalent, then learn on the job or through an apprenticeship that combines technical training with paid work, often sponsored by unions and contractor associations.
    Credentials to know
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred
    • Forklift operatorFederal rule
    • NCCEREmployer-preferred

    Source: BLS — Carpenters

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Facilities Maintenance

    Repair & maintain buildings

    The work
    Keep buildings and equipment working, from painting and flooring to plumbing, electrical, and heating and cooling systems.
    How to start
    A high school diploma or equivalent. Most learn on the job, starting with simple repairs and taking on harder work alongside experienced workers. Some complete apprenticeships.
    Credentials to know
    • EPA 608Federal rule
    • CAMTEmployer-preferred
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred

    Source: BLS — General maintenance and repair workers

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • Heavy Equipment

    Operate jobsite machinery

    The work
    Operate the heavy machinery used to build roads, buildings, and other structures.
    How to start
    A high school diploma or equivalent. Many start on light equipment under an experienced operator. Others complete a 3- or 4-year apprenticeship or a vocational program.
    Credentials to know
    • CDL Class A / BState / local
    • NCCEREmployer-preferred
    • OSHA 10 / 30Employer-preferred

    Source: BLS — Construction equipment operators

    Explore the trade →Resume guide

  • General Labor

    Start building field experience

    The work
    Support crews by handling materials and tools, keeping sites and equipment clean, and assisting skilled tradespeople.
    How to start
    Usually no formal credential is required. Most learn on the job by working alongside experienced workers, and some go on to apprenticeships sponsored by unions or trade associations.
    Credentials to know
    • OSHA 10Employer-preferred
    • Forklift operatorFederal rule
    • CDLState / local

    Source: BLS — Construction laborers and helpers

YOUR FIRST STEPS

Picked a lane? Here's how to get in.

No one starts as a journeyman. These are the common ways in, with links to the official sources so you can check the rules where you live.

  1. 01

    Learn the basics

    Most trades ask for a high school diploma or equivalent. Shop classes, technical schools, and community colleges teach the fundamentals, and in some trades that schooling counts toward an apprenticeship.

    Compare the trades →
  2. 02

    Look for a paid apprenticeship

    A Registered Apprenticeship is a job: you earn a wage that rises as your skills grow, learn on the job from an experienced mentor, take related classroom training, and finish with a nationally recognized credential.

    Search the Apprenticeship Job Finder ↗

    Source: Apprenticeship.gov — Registered Apprenticeship

  3. 03

    Earn your first credentials

    Know what each one actually is before you put it on an application.

    • OSHA 10-Hour and 30-Hour Outreach TrainingTraining card · Employer-preferred
    • EPA Section 608 Technician CertificationCertification · Federal rule
    • Forklift (Powered Industrial Truck) Operator TrainingQualification · Federal rule
    • Commercial Driver's License (CDL)License · State / local
  4. 04

    Check your state's rules

    Licenses for electricians, plumbers, and some HVAC and welding work are set by states and localities. Look up what your state requires before you plan your training.

    Use the CareerOneStop License Finder ↗
